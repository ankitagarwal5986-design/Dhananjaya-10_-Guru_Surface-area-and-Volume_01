Class 10 CBSE Chapter Mastery — Surface Areas and Volumes

📐 Master Formula Compendium: Solid Shapes

Comprehensive breakdown of dimensional parameters, lateral/curved surfaces (CSA), total surface areas (TSA), and cubic capacities (Volume) for standard three-dimensional geometric figures.

1. Cuboid (Rectangular Prism)

Length \(l\), Width \(b\), Height \(h\)

Lateral Surface (LSA): \(2h(l + b)\)

Total Surface (TSA): \(2(lb + bh + hl)\)

Volume (\(V\)): \(l \times b \times h\)

Diagonal (\(d\)): \(\sqrt{l^2 + b^2 + h^2}\)

2. Cube (Regular Hexahedron)

Side / Edge length \(a\)

Lateral Surface (LSA): \(4a^2\)

Total Surface (TSA): \(6a^2\)

Volume (\(V\)): \(a^3\)

Diagonal (\(d\)): \(a\sqrt{3}\)

3. Right Circular Cylinder

Radius \(r\), Height \(h\)

Curved Surface (CSA): \(2\pi rh\)

Total Surface (TSA): \(2\pi r(r + h)\)

Volume (\(V\)): \(\pi r^2 h\)

End Cap Bases (2): \(2\pi r^2\)

4. Right Circular Cone

Radius \(r\), Height \(h\), Slant \(l\)

Slant Height (\(l\)): \(\sqrt{r^2 + h^2}\)

Curved Surface (CSA): \(\pi r l\)

Total Surface (TSA): \(\pi r(r + l)\)

Volume (\(V\)): \(\frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h\)

5. Sphere

Radius \(r\)

Curved Surface (CSA): \(4\pi r^2\)

Total Surface (TSA): \(4\pi r^2\)

Volume (\(V\)): \(\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3\)

Cross-Section: \(\pi r^2\)

6. Hemisphere

Radius \(r\)

Curved Surface (CSA): \(2\pi r^2\)

Total Surface (TSA): \(3\pi r^2\)

Volume (\(V\)): \(\frac{2}{3}\pi r^3\)

Circular Rim Base: \(\pi r^2\)

Core Concept I

1. Surface Area Principles for Combined Solids

When two solids are joined, their common interfaces disappear inside the body[cite: 4]. The total surface area (TSA) of the composite solid is the sum of the curved/exposed surface areas of each component[cite: 4].

Key Formulas for Combination Solids: \begin{align*} \text{Toy (Hemisphere + Cone):} \quad \text{TSA} &= \text{CSA of Hemisphere} + \text{CSA of Cone} = 2\pi r^2 + \pi rl \text{[cite: 4]} \\ \text{Capsule (Cylinder + 2 Hemispheres):} \quad \text{TSA} &= 2\pi rh + 2(2\pi r^2) = 2\pi rh + 4\pi r^2 \text{[cite: 4]} \\ \text{Cube with Hemispherical Surmount/Depression:} \quad \text{TSA} &= 6a^2 - \pi r^2 + 2\pi r^2 = 6a^2 + \pi r^2 \text{[cite: 4]} \end{align*}
Core Concept II

2. Volume Principles for Combined Solids

Volume is an additive physical property[cite: 4]. The total volume of a combined solid is the sum of the volumes of its constituents[cite: 4]. For hollowed-out cavities, the remaining volume is found by subtraction[cite: 4].

Key Formulas for Combined Volumes: \begin{align*} \text{Cone standing on Hemisphere:} \quad V &= \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h + \frac{2}{3}\pi r^3 = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 (h + 2r) \text{[cite: 4]} \\ \text{Cylinder with 2 Conical Ends:} \quad V &= \pi r^2 h_{\text{cyl}} + 2\left(\frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h_{\text{cone}}\right) \text{[cite: 4]} \\ \text{Solid with Cavity Scooped Out:} \quad V_{\text{remaining}} &= V_{\text{outer}} - V_{\text{cavity}} \text{[cite: 4]} \end{align*}
